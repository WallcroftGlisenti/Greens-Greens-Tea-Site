III · Rituals

The Pour

Three things matter more than the leaf: the warming of the pot, the temper of the water, and the willingness to wait.

01

Warm the pot

Fill it with hot water while the kettle works. Pour that water away only when you are ready to brew — a cold pot steals the first minute of the leaf.

02

Measure the leaf

A level spoon for each cup, and one for the pot. Adjust by a leaf rather than a scoop; the tea will tell you which way to go.

03

Pour the whole

Three minutes, perhaps four. Then pour all of it. A leaf left standing turns bitter, and a pot left half-full sulks.

IV · The Journal

Notes from the House

Kept short, written by hand, and posted later than intended.

No. I

On the First Pour

The first pour of the morning is not really about tea. It is about deciding, before the day has asked anything of you, that one thing will be done slowly and well.

No. II

The Weight of a Leaf

A spoon is a poor measure and a good beginning. The hand learns what the scale knows: that a little more leaf is usually the kinder error.

Inquiries are answered in the order received, which is to say: slowly.

The Tea Ritual

Notes for the table

  1. 01

    Warm the pot

    Fill the pot with hot water while the kettle comes up, and empty it only at the last moment. A warmed pot holds the heat of the pour; a cold one takes it.

  2. 02

    Measure the leaf

    A level spoon for each cup and one for the pot is the old instruction, and a good place to begin. Adjust by a single leaf rather than a scoop; the tea will tell you which way to go.

  3. 03

    Temper the water

    Just short of a boil for green, a full rolling boil for black and oolong. Let the kettle rest a moment after it sings. The leaf asks for heat, not violence.

  4. 04

    Give it time

    Three minutes, perhaps four. Then pour the whole of it. A leaf left standing turns bitter, and a pot left half-full sulks. Both are avoidable.

The pot is warmed first. Always the pot.
